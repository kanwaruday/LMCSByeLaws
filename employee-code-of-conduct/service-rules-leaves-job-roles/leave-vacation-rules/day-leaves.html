Day Leaves

Day-based leave categories cover full-day absences. Each has different approval requirements and salary implications.

Single Leave (Unpaid)

A Single Leave is one unpaid working day off. It is granted when an employee has exhausted their Casual Leave balance for the year.

  • Treated as an unpaid day — one day's salary is deducted.
  • Requires prior written application and Principal's approval.
  • If the leave is sandwiched around a public holiday (i.e., taken the day before or after a Sunday/gazetted holiday), the holiday is also counted as a Single Leave (sandwich rule). The sandwiched day becomes unpaid as well.

Double Leave

Double Leave is a teachers-only cap mechanism: not more than 10% of a branch's teaching staff may be on leave on a given day, rounded down. Once that cap is reached, the next teacher to apply is marked Double Leave — 2 leave days deducted for that one day's absence, rather than Absent.

  • Worked example: a branch with 24 teachers has a cap of ⌊24 ÷ 10⌋ = 2. If 2 teachers are already on leave that day, a 3rd teacher applying is marked Double Leave.
  • This is separate from Special Day Leave, which despite a similar-sounding "2 days" figure is a different rule entirely (missing a Special Day despite 7+ days' notice) — the two are not related and the terms shouldn't be used interchangeably.
  • Long Leave is excluded. Consecutive absences exceeding 6 working days are automatically classified as Long Leave and do not count toward the 10% cap. Long Leave requires MD Operations approval and may affect service continuity.

Absent

An employee is marked "Absent" when they fail to report to duty without prior approval and without notifying the school.

Rule Details
Leave equivalent 1 Absent = 2 unpaid leave days deducted from salary
Notification deadline Must be notified through the Online Portal by 7:00 AM the day of the absence (updated 2026-09-16 per OO-09/26 — previously 6:00 PM the evening before). Failure to notify by this deadline = Absent, regardless of the reason
Break in service 7 or more consecutive Absent days without communication = break in service. The employee may be treated as having abandoned their post, and disciplinary proceedings will be initiated
Employees who are marked Absent repeatedly (more than 3 times in a term) will be counselled by the Principal. A fourth instance in the same term may result in a formal show-cause notice.
